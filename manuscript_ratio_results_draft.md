# Juvenile:Adult-female Ratio Results

Because the demographic count matrix is labeled `juvenile`, these results are framed as a camera-based juvenile:adult-female composition index rather than a strict fawn recruitment estimate. Across all camera-rounds with finite counts for both classes, the pooled juvenile:adult-female ratio was `0.102`, equivalent to `10.2` juveniles per `100` adult females. Round-specific ratios were low and relatively stable through the first four rounds (`6.8` to `8.8` juveniles per `100` females in `X1` to `X4`), then increased in later rounds to `11.8` in `X5`, `16.5` in `X6`, and `35.9` in `X7`. This temporal pattern indicates strong seasonal change in demographic composition across the survey window, although the final round should be interpreted cautiously because the female denominator was small (`39` adult females).

At the camera x round level, a beta-binomial mixed model of juvenile versus adult-female composition showed that habitat openness was the only clearly supported fixed effect. `Open.Spaces` was positively associated with juvenile representation among identified juveniles and adult females (odds ratio `1.70`, 95% CI `1.02` to `2.82`, `p = 0.042`). No predator or human covariate was clearly associated with the juvenile:adult-female composition index, and the round effects themselves were positive but imprecise relative to the first round. These results suggest that juveniles were proportionally more common at more open camera stations, while temporal variation in the ratio was stronger descriptively than inferentially once site-level heterogeneity was modeled.

Taken together, the composition analysis supports the broader class-specific inference from the abundance models: juvenile gazelles were most strongly associated with open habitat. However, this ratio-based analysis should be interpreted as a standardized demographic composition index, not as a direct estimate of recruitment or fawn survival.
